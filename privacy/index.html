Privacy Policy

Soundtrail keeps your music library on your device by default. Optional online features — provider connections, a model reading, voice recognition, the sync server, map tiles, and exports — each have their own data flow, explained below with the exact endpoint involved.

1. Who we are and what this covers

Soundtrail is an Android app, package tech.egrie.soundtrail, for saving music to places, creating playlists, using optional location, motion, and time automations, building mood mixes, asking for music by voice or text, and browsing the library in a car. It is maintained under the egrie-tech project by an operator based in Ontario, Canada. In this policy, “we” and “us” mean Everett Grieve (egrie-tech, also known as mob5824m-wq), Ontario, Canada. Privacy contact: mob5824@proton.me.

This policy covers the app and the default Soundtrail sync/audio server we operate. A fresh install uses our server by default: the app falls back to https://soundtrail.duckdns.org whenever the Sync server URL field is left empty, and you may instead enter a self-hosted server address. The default service is operated by the Soundtrail operator identified above and self-hosted in Canada, and it is identified in the app on the Connect → Cloud sync screen, where a Use public server button restores that address. The endpoint is reached only after you create or sign in to an account or enable sync. Amazon Web Services (AWS) is a possible future hosting provider, not a current provider for our server. We will update this policy before a hosting change that materially affects how or where your information is handled. When you use an independently hosted Soundtrail server or a Jellyfin server, its operator controls its server-held information and its own policy also applies.

Optional model readings and speech recognition are provided by third parties you choose or that your phone already uses; those services are described in section 5. They are not operated by us, and their own terms and privacy policies apply when you use them. See the provider policy links for common services; user-entered endpoints and self-hosted servers may have different operators.

2. Information stored on your device

Soundtrail processes and stores the information you add: pin titles, artists, place labels, notes, music links, coordinates, creation times, playlist names and entries, and automation rules. Imported and downloaded audio is copied to private app storage. Connection settings, account labels, mood descriptions you type or speak when they become a mood reading, an optional energy hint, learned genre/mood tags, and model preferences are also stored to support the features you enable. Speech audio is never stored; a spoken request is not saved as a transcript, except that words used as a mood description are saved locally with the Mood tab’s settings.

These library records stay on your device by default. Enabling cloud sync sends an encrypted version of supported library records to your selected server; exporting or sharing sends information to a destination you choose. Local audio, music-provider credentials, mood settings, learned tags, and model API keys are not part of cloud sync and are excluded from JSON exports.

Soundtrail does not use an advertising ID or run advertising. The only third-party service SDK in the app’s dependency list is Google’s sign-in library, used solely for the optional YouTube connection; there is no general-purpose app-analytics or third-party crash-reporting SDK. Speech recognition and text-to-speech are Android platform services supplied by the phone, not SDKs bundled by Soundtrail. Mood models are called over ordinary HTTP(S) requests, not through a bundled model SDK. Google authentication-related usage information is discussed below. We do not sell personal information or use your library or Google user data for advertising. Separate music, model, and speech providers have their own practices, which are not controlled by this app.

3. Location, motion, voice, and Android permissions

With your permission, Soundtrail uses approximate or precise device location to place pins and evaluate place-based automations. Its optional automation watch runs as a foreground location service with an ongoing notification and may continue when the app is not on screen. Motion categories such as walking, running, or driving are estimated on-device from GPS speed bands; the app does not use an Activity Recognition API or any advertising identifier for this, or for anything else.

Saved pin coordinates and automation locations are stored locally and included in encrypted cloud sync if you enable it. Map requests can reveal the area you view, as described below. The app does not send a continuous raw GPS movement history to the sync service as part of its documented sync protocol.

The optional Ask tab uses the microphone. Android’s RECORD_AUDIO permission is requested at the moment you press the microphone button, not at launch, and only while a request is being spoken. Soundtrail does not record, store, or upload the audio itself; the phone’s chosen speech-recognition service handles it and returns recognised words. The app also queries the device for speech-recognition and text-to-speech services so those buttons appear only when the phone has them. Section 5 explains what those services may receive.

You can turn off the automation watch, refuse the microphone, or revoke location or microphone permission in Android settings. Some features will then be unavailable; typing works without the microphone, and the mood engine works without a model. Notification permission supports automation alerts and the playback controls of the in-app player. Music plays through a media-playback foreground service with a MediaStyle notification, which is how a stream continues while the screen is off; that service runs only while you are actually playing something. Internet access supports online features. The optional Google connection can use Android account permission to find Google-type accounts on your device. Limited installed-app checks detect supported music apps, Google/microG services, and speech engines for handoff, connection setup, and voice availability; they are not a general inventory of installed apps.

To let a car browse the library, Soundtrail exports an Android media-browser service. The car runs in another app, so the service must be reachable, but it answers only the app itself, the known car hosts (Android Auto and Android Automotive), the Google app, the system media UI, and callers the platform has already trusted with media control (MEDIA_CONTENT_CONTROL) — identified by the uid the system reports, not by a name a caller claims. Other callers receive no library root. Section 5 describes what the car can see.

4. Optional music-service connections

Only connect a service if you want its features. Requests to a provider expose your IP address and ordinary connection metadata, in addition to the information described here. A connection you have already established can also be refreshed in the background when the app opens — a stored provider profile, or your YouTube playlists while auto-import is on — so disconnecting is the action that stops those requests, not closing the app. Providers handle data under their own terms and privacy policies.

  • Spotify: OAuth with PKCE authorizes track search. Search text and authenticated API requests go to api.spotify.com, including the per-track request used to read its short preview. Where a preview exists it plays in Soundtrail’s own player; full-length tracks still open in the Spotify app or a browser. Access and refresh credentials are stored encrypted on-device using Android Keystore.
  • SoundCloud: OAuth 2.1 with PKCE authorizes search. SoundCloud receives search text, authentication requests, and the stream-resolution requests that produce an in-app playable URL. Tokens and the configured client secret are stored encrypted on-device. When SoundCloud has no stream to offer, the pin link simply opens in the SoundCloud app or a browser.
  • Jellyfin: Your chosen server receives sign-in credentials, music-library searches, authenticated streaming and background-playback requests, and authorized download requests, each capped at 250 MiB per track. Soundtrail does not store the Jellyfin password; its access token is stored encrypted on-device. Library records use item references rather than embedding the server address or token in backups or sync.
  • TheAudioDB: Artist and song-title lookup sends that text to TheAudioDB’s public v1 API, using the provider’s documented shared free key, for music metadata. It requires no user account and stores nothing about the lookup. Results can be used to fill a pin. If you separately switch on Look up genre & mood tags, the artist and title of the specific untagged songs a mood mix is unsure about are also sent there, capped per run; the tags learned from the reply are cached locally. That switch is off by default.
  • YouTube Music handoff: Search or library actions open the installed music app or website. Search text or song links go to that app or website. Soundtrail does not read the music app’s private storage or session.

Google and YouTube API data

If you explicitly connect Google, Soundtrail requests your email and the YouTube youtube.readonly scope and retrieves metadata for videos in your own YouTube playlists through the official YouTube Data API. No YouTube write scope is requested, and this does not provide access to the entire YouTube Music library — saved songs, albums, artists, and Music-only mixes stay in the YouTube Music app. Two mutually exclusive sign-in methods are offered, and they store different things:

  • Google account, which requires Google Play services or a standard microG installation to be present on the device, because the sign-in is performed by that service under the com.google.android.gms package name. MicroG RE and ReVanced GmsCore authenticate patched apps only and therefore cannot serve this path; on those setups the Client ID option is the available one. Here Soundtrail asks for the Android Accounts permission to find the Google-type account on your device so it can connect that account without a picker. If you deny it, or there is not exactly one such account, the ordinary Google sign-in screen opens instead. Nothing from this path is written to disk; see the next paragraph.
  • Desktop OAuth Client ID in the browser, for setups with no Google services (including MicroG RE): Google issues an access token and a refresh token for your own client ID, and Soundtrail stores both, plus an expiry, encrypted with Android Keystore — the same treatment as Spotify and SoundCloud credentials — refreshing them as needed. This is a persisted grant, so revoking it in your Google account is a meaningful step, not a formality.

Importing playlists is on by default once a connection is established and can be switched off in Connect. The import is deliberately bounded (at most 200 playlists and 2,000 items each) and writes those entries into your local library, so imported titles and links become ordinary local records that can then be exported or encrypted-synced like anything else you saved. Re-importing is idempotent: only newly found items are appended, and the import consolidates into one local liked list — a song already present under the same link is not added twice, and an offline copy stays attached. Search over the playlists is also bounded — the first 50 playlists and the first 50 items in each — and that search text is matched on-device against the locally held metadata rather than being sent to YouTube.

Separately, the Ask tab can search YouTube’s music catalogue when a Google account is connected. That action sends the request’s search phrase to the YouTube Data API — the same kind of query as a YouTube search — and returns matching public videos; it does not read YouTube Music’s account library, saved songs, albums, or likes. See section 5 for the voice path and section 8 for playing a result.

On the Google-account path the access token is requested from Google services or compatible microG when needed, used in memory for the API call, and is not persisted by Soundtrail or sent to its sync/audio server; only the selected account name, type, and display label are stored, encrypted on-device. Google and microG may independently manage account credentials. The Client ID path persists its own tokens as described above, and neither path stores anything on the sync server.

Google authentication analytics: Google’s authentication services may process sign-in activity, service-usage information, and diagnostic metadata under the Google Privacy Policy. Authentication-related usage information made available to us is used to monitor the Google connection, not to profile your movements or music listening. We do not send pin coordinates, notes, or audio files to Google for analytics. This is separate from the playlist metadata you authorize Soundtrail to retrieve.

Google user data is used only to provide the playlist and catalogue-search features you request, not for advertising, sale, or general-purpose AI training. It is not made available to people for routine inspection. Transfers of saved entries occur through your requested exports or encrypted sync. Soundtrail’s use and transfer of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements.

Disconnecting the YouTube connection clears Soundtrail’s stored tokens, account references, imported playlist metadata, and memory cache, and on the Client ID path asks Google to revoke the grant on a best-effort basis. You can also revoke access through your Google account’s third-party connections. Disconnecting a provider does not automatically delete songs you previously saved or data the provider retains.

5. Voice, speech recognition, and optional AI mood readings

These features are optional. The local library, the on-device mood engine, typed requests, and Android Auto browsing work without either of them. Both are off or idle unless you use them.

Voice input and spoken replies

When you press the microphone in the Ask tab, Android asks for microphone permission at that moment. Soundtrail passes the request to the phone’s selected speech-recognition service and receives the recognised words; it does not record, store, or upload the audio itself, and it does not save a transcript — except that a request used as a mood description is stored locally with the Mood tab’s settings, as section 2 describes. Depending on the service and the phone, recognition may run on-device or be processed on the provider’s servers. A standard Google recogniser can send the audio to Google under the Google Privacy Policy; an offline engine does not. The app cannot control which engine the phone uses, and the engine’s own settings and policy govern its handling. Typing a request avoids the microphone and the speech service entirely.

For a song request, if a Google account is connected, Ask sends the recognised phrase (not the audio) to the YouTube Data API to search the music catalogue, unless the request names another provider such as Spotify or SoundCloud. Matching against your own library happens on-device, but the YouTube lookup is still a separate network request. If a result is played, its video ID is sent to your sync server for audio extraction as described in section 8.

Ask can speak its answer through the phone’s text-to-speech engine. The words are the app’s reply — a mood label, a song count, an explanation — not your library. Many engines synthesize on-device; a network voice may send the text to the engine provider under its own policy. The switch on the Ask card turns spoken replies off, and the app stores no audio.

AI mood readings (off by default)

AI Mood is for music discovery only, not medical or mental-health advice, diagnosis, or crisis support. Avoid including sensitive or identifying details in mood descriptions, especially if you enable a third-party model.

The Mood tab’s default reading is a small on-device engine; it needs no network, account, or key, and your description, library, and mix are not uploaded for it. If you switch on a model, you choose one of these endpoints:

  • Ollama at an address you enter — a server on your phone, your network, or hardware you rent. The request goes only to that address.
  • An OpenAI-compatible endpoint at an address you enter. This covers OpenAI, OpenRouter, a model on your own machine, a bridge in front of another service, or Arena’s gateway through the built-in preset.
  • Google AI Studio (Gemini) with an API key you provide.

What leaves the device for a reading is limited to: the mood description you typed (up to 400 characters), an energy hint if you set one, the local hour, whether you are moving (only while the automation watch is on), and the sentence naming where to search (“find music on YouTube that fits: …”). Like any network request, it also carries the endpoint’s API key when one is used, the model name you selected, your IP address, and ordinary request metadata. Song titles, artists, playlists, pins, places, and the contents of your library are not sent. The selected endpoint may log or retain the request, use it under its own improvement or training terms, charge for use, or process it in another country. Changing the provider or endpoint URL can change who receives the request and which policies apply. For user-entered endpoints, Soundtrail cannot verify who operates them or which policies apply. Before enabling or changing an endpoint, verify the provider and URL, review its current Privacy Policy, Terms of Service, retention/training practices, and any charges, and follow its applicable terms and usage policies. The provider controls its service and data handling, and we do not receive its logs.

If you choose an endpoint that speaks plain HTTP on your local network, that traffic is not encrypted. The app accepts http:// for a plainly local address and assumes HTTPS for a public host; you can type a scheme explicitly. Use HTTPS for any endpoint you do not control. The endpoint’s key, and an optional Ollama proxy token, are encrypted with Android Keystore, never synced, excluded from exports, and removed by Remove. A connection test may send one short sample reading to the endpoint; on Arena, the app says so because Arena’s usage page records prompts.

A model’s reply is a suggestion. The app searches each named title and artist on the provider you selected — YouTube, Spotify, SoundCloud, or Jellyfin — or matches it against your own library before showing or playing a row, and that lookup sends the search phrase to the provider. If the target is YouTube Music, which publishes no search API for this app, a row is instead a link that opens the song or a search in the YouTube Music app, which may send the query when opened. A song that cannot be found is left out rather than invented. Turning the model off, or selecting On-device, stops all model requests.

6. Map tiles and network metadata

The map loads OpenStreetMap-based raster tiles from CARTO’s public tile service at basemaps.cartocdn.com. CARTO receives your IP address, tile coordinates, zoom level, request timing, and a static Soundtrail User-Agent string. Tile coordinates reveal the visible map area, which can include your current or saved location. Individual pin records, notes, and exact pin coordinates are rendered locally and are not added to tile requests. Tiles are cached in memory.

Although we do not retain server access/request logs on our default service, third-party music services, model providers, speech-recognition and text-to-speech services, Google authentication services, network providers, and independent server operators may record IP addresses and request metadata. Their logging, retention, and processing locations depend on their own practices. Soundtrail’s local-first design does not mean that online features make no network disclosures.

7. Default server and self-hosted encrypted sync

Our server is the default connection endpoint; you can choose a self-hosted endpoint instead. A connection to a server exposes your IP address and connection timing even before library content is uploaded. If you register or sign in, the selected server processes your email address and password for account creation and login. The supplied server implementation stores per-account salted scrypt password hashes, SHA-256 hashes of random session tokens that expire after 30 days, encrypted sync records, event IDs and sequences, retry-receipt metadata, and related account/session metadata. It applies a 64 MiB per-account quota to encrypted events and checkpoints, an 8 MiB snapshot cap, and in-process rate limits on account creation, login, sync reads and writes, checkpoints, deletion, and extraction. It has no email verification, no password-reset service, and no key-recovery endpoint. Its host or reverse proxy may log IP addresses and request timing. Automatic sync is off by default; when you enable it, a sync runs at app launch and never while the app is closed. See the self-host guide for the exact limits an operator should expect to configure.

Pins (including coordinates and notes), playlist metadata, music links, and automation rules are encrypted on your device using AES-256-GCM before upload. The server stores encrypted events and checkpoints and can see their sizes and timing, but does not receive the plaintext library content or recovery key through this protocol. Local audio files, music-provider credentials, mood settings, learned tags, and model keys are excluded.

You control your recovery key. Losing it can make encrypted information unrecoverable on every device: changing the account password cannot recover it, and a reinstall removes this device’s stored copy, so save the key before you rely on sync. End-to-end encryption of library content does not hide your account email, IP address, or traffic metadata. Your chosen server operator’s privacy and security practices remain important.

8. Optional YouTube audio extraction

This feature is separate from the official read-only YouTube API and is not end-to-end encrypted. After you confirm you own the content or have permission to download it, Soundtrail sends the video ID and the request under your authenticated Soundtrail server account. The server contacts YouTube and temporarily processes the audio before transferring it to your device. The same path prepares a YouTube song for in-app playback when it is found by Ask or a mood mix, or opened from a YouTube pin; the rights confirmation still applies.

Extraction accepts exactly one YouTube video ID per request; playlists and general video downloads are not supported. The supplied server does not use Google OAuth tokens, YouTube login cookies, or a YouTube Music session, and it performs the download with the third-party yt-dlp project and FFmpeg, so it can stop working when YouTube changes. Temporary audio sits in a per-request directory and is removed when the response finishes or fails in normal operation. Crashes, host backups, and server logs may require separate operator cleanup and retention rules. The resulting audio stays in private app storage and is not cloud-synced.

9. Exports, imported files, and sharing

A JSON export contains pins, coordinates, notes, playlists, song links, and automation rules. It is a readable, unencrypted file, unlike encrypted sync. Login credentials, model API keys, mood settings, and audio bytes are excluded. You choose where to save it; a file-storage provider you choose may receive it. Protect exported files and delete copies you no longer need.

Imports read the file you select and merge supported records into your library. Audio import copies the selected audio into app storage. Playback or link sharing can grant the player or app you choose access to the selected audio or link. Soundtrail does not control what the receiving app does with that information.

10. Retention and deletion

  • On your device: Library records remain until you delete them or clear app data/uninstall. You can remove pins, playlist entries, and automations, and manage individual offline audio files or clear all offline copies. Mood descriptions, preferences, and learned tags remain in app storage until you change them, clear app data, or uninstall; model API keys are removed with Remove in the Mood tab. Removing a song or playlist removes its attached app-managed audio copy, not your original source file. Speech audio is never retained; a spoken mood description is retained with the mood settings described above.
  • Provider connections: Disconnect within Soundtrail to remove local connection credentials. Revoke grants or manage provider-held data with the provider separately.
  • Model and speech services: Requests you send to a model endpoint, a speech-recognition service, or a text-to-speech service are retained by that provider under its own policy; we do not receive or control those records. Deleting the app or removing a key does not delete copies held by a third party.
  • Sync: Disconnecting sync does not delete the remote account. Use the cloud-sync account deletion action to delete the account, sessions, encrypted events, receipts, and checkpoint from the supplied server’s active database. This leaves the device library untouched. Deletions of individual records propagate when successfully synced; encrypted deletion markers (tombstones) and history may remain until checkpoint compaction or account deletion, and compaction can retire the retry-receipt metadata that would otherwise reject a replayed event ID.
  • Other copies: Exported backups, libraries on other devices, provider records, and server backup/log copies are not automatically erased by deleting an app record or remote account. Manage those separately.

We do not retain server access/request logs on our self-hosted default service. This does not mean that the server stores no data: account, session, and encrypted sync records described above remain while needed to provide your account, until expiry, compaction, or account deletion as applicable. Google authentication-related information is handled separately as described in section 4; Google controls retention of its own records. The supplied server software does not automatically create database backups. If we introduce a managed backup process, we will disclose its retention and deletion rules. If you cannot sign in to delete an account, email mob5824@proton.me; we will help after proportionate identity verification. We may retain limited information where required by law and will explain any applicable exception to a deletion request. If you contact us, we use the information you send to respond to your request, provide support, or resolve a privacy concern. We retain it only as long as reasonably necessary for that purpose and any applicable legal obligations. For independently hosted servers, ask that operator about retention and deletion.

11. Security and processing locations

Soundtrail uses app-private storage, Android Keystore-backed encryption for stored connection secrets and model API keys, and on-device encryption for sync payloads. Android automatic app backup is disabled in the reviewed build. Not all local library records, mood settings, or exported backups are separately encrypted. No system can guarantee complete security.

Use HTTPS for sync and provider connections. Jellyfin can be configured over plain HTTP on a trusted local network; HTTP can expose credentials and traffic to others on that network. The app also accepts a plainly local http:// address for a self-hosted model endpoint, because a model server on your own network often has no TLS; use HTTPS for any endpoint you do not control. Secure your device, recovery key, exports, API keys, and self-hosted servers.

Online providers or your selected host may process information in countries other than yours, where legal protections differ. On-device processing takes place on your device. The processing location for optional sync, audio extraction, a model reading, or speech recognition depends on the server, model endpoint, or speech engine you select. Consult that operator and each music provider about their locations and safeguards before connecting. Our location in Ontario does not mean all provider or server processing takes place in Canada.

12. Your choices and privacy rights

You can use local features without enabling cloud sync or connecting every provider, control Android permissions, stop the automation watch, refuse the microphone and type instead, turn spoken replies off, return the Mood tab to On-device, remove model API keys, export your library, and use the deletion choices above. These choices may limit particular features.

Depending on your location, you may have rights to access, correct, delete, receive a portable copy of, object to, or restrict certain processing, withdraw consent, and complain to a privacy regulator. Contact mob5824@proton.me for operator-held information. We may need proportionate verification of your identity. We cannot retrieve data stored only on your device or decrypt sync content without the user-held key; please do not send us that key. Requests about a model provider, a speech engine, or a music service must go to that provider.

For information under our control, we will handle access and correction requests, consent withdrawals, and privacy complaints in accordance with applicable Canadian privacy law, including the Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (PIPEDA) where it applies. Withdrawal of consent does not undo processing already lawfully performed and may prevent us from providing a requested feature. Where applicable, you may complain to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada. Other mandatory rights may apply where you live. Send requests about independent servers or music-provider records to their operators.

13. Children and age requirements

Soundtrail’s services are intended for adults aged 18 or older who have reached the age of majority where they live; they are not directed at children. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children through services we operate. If you believe a child has provided information to us, contact the privacy contact below so we can investigate and delete information under our control where appropriate. Local device records and independently operated server accounts must be managed on that device or with that server operator. This policy statement is not a claim that the app technically verifies age.
