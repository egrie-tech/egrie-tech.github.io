Open source · Android · local-first

Your music, pinned to
the places you love.

Soundtrail saves Spotify, SoundCloud, YouTube Music, and Jellyfin songs as private pins on your own map — with local playlists, offline audio, mood mixes, voice or typed requests, opt-in place/motion/time automations, Android Auto, and end-to-end encrypted sync you can self-host. Everything stays on your device unless you turn a connection on.

Features

What the app does

Each feature below is opt-in except the local library. Nothing described here requires a Soundtrail account or a server; some optional connections need their own provider account or key.

Map

Pins on your own map

An in-app OpenStreetMap-based map draws every pin in its provider colour. Tap for the song card, long-press anywhere to drop a new pin, zoom and pan, centre on yourself, or find a soundspot by name. Switch List ↔ Map at any time.

Providers

Your existing music, not a new store

Search Spotify, SoundCloud, your Jellyfin server, or your own YouTube playlists, or paste a supported link. YouTube imports — including Liked videos and Uploads — land in one local liked list rather than piling up copies.

Playback

One player and one queue

Jellyfin streams, your imported files, Spotify previews, SoundCloud streams, YouTube audio prepared by your own server, and songs found by Ask or Mood all play through the same player, notification, lockscreen and car controls. Full-length Spotify tracks still open in Spotify's own app.

Offline

Audio you can keep

Attach an audio file you already own, or download an eligible Jellyfin track through its authenticated endpoint (250 MiB per track). Offline storage is yours to audit: per-file sizes, largest first, remove one or clear all.

Automations

Playlists that notice where you are

Point a playlist at conditions: near a saved spot (20–2000 m), while walking, running, or driving, and/or between two times of day. An opt-in foreground watch posts a notification when a rule matches — it never plays audio without a tap.

Mood

Describe a feeling, get a mix

The on-device engine reads how you feel and scores the songs in your own pins and playlists; nothing is sent. Optional and off by default: TheAudioDB genre/mood tags, or a model reading from Ollama on hardware you control, any OpenAI-compatible endpoint (including Arena's preset, whose free-router keys are restricted to Arena's own coding harnesses), or Google AI Studio with your own key.

Ask

Say it, or type it

The Ask tab takes a request by voice or keyboard. Your library answers first, on-device. With a Google account connected it also searches YouTube's music catalogue, and every result joins the same queue. Replies can be spoken through the phone's text-to-speech engine, and the switch turns that off.

Car

Android Auto, with your own library

Plug in and Soundtrail appears in the car's media launcher with your pins and playlists. Play, pause, next, previous and the scrubber all act on the one queue; on-device voice search matches only what is already saved. The car service answers known media hosts, never arbitrary apps.

Sync

Encrypted, and yours to host

Optional two-way sync of pins, playlists, links, and automations, encrypted on-device with AES-256-GCM under a recovery key only you hold. The public server works out of the box; a self-hosted server takes one URL change.

Portability

Plain-JSON backup

Export pins, coordinates, notes, playlists, song links, and automation rules to a JSON file you choose; import merges them back additively. No proprietary cloud format, no lock-in.

Sharing

Share-back from your music app

Share a song from Spotify, YouTube Music (including Morphe), or SoundCloud into Soundtrail and the pin editor opens prefilled. Soundtrail never reads another app's storage or session.

Under the hood

Works on a de-Googled phone

Motion is estimated from GPS speed rather than an activity-recognition API, the whole local library works with the network switched off, and installing needs no Play Store account. One optional feature wants Google services: the Google-account sign-in for YouTube playlist search, which needs Play services or standard microG. The Client ID option skips them entirely, the on-device mood engine needs nothing, and voice input uses whichever speech engine the phone has — typing works without one.

Your data

Three paths, and you control each one

The local library is the default and the whole product. The optional paths that move information — provider connections, a model reading, and cloud sync — are separate switches with different data flows. Voice is handled by the phone's speech engine, and car playback exposes the library only to the car.

Stays on the device

  • Pins, titles, artists, notes, coordinates, place labels, and creation times.
  • Playlists, imported audio, and downloaded offline files, in private app storage.
  • Automation rules, the live watch state, mood descriptions and learned tags (unless you switch on a model, which sends only the description and coarse context), and provider or model credentials (Android Keystore-encrypted).
  • Search matching against your YouTube playlists and the car's voice search, both of which happen on-device.
  • The words the speech recognizer returns are used for the request in front of you; Soundtrail does not save a transcript, apart from a mood description you use to build a mix, which stays with your local mood settings.

Leaves the device only when you enable it

  • Provider search/playback: your query, track IDs, and ordinary request metadata reach Spotify, SoundCloud, TheAudioDB, Google's YouTube Data API, or your Jellyfin server.
  • A model reading: only your mood description, an energy hint, the local hour, whether you are moving, and the search-target sentence reach the endpoint you chose, along with its key, the model name, and ordinary request metadata — never song titles, playlists, pins, or places.
  • Voice: the phone's chosen speech service handles the audio, which may be recognised on-device or by its provider; spoken replies go to the phone's text-to-speech engine.
  • Ask: for a song request, with a Google account connected, the search phrase goes to YouTube's Data API. Playing a YouTube result sends that video ID to your sync server for extraction, exactly like any other YouTube pin.
  • Cloud sync: encrypted library records reach the server you selected; your recovery key and audio files never do.
  • Map tiles: the visible area reaches the tile server, because tiles are fetched for the area you look at.
  • Exports: a readable JSON file goes wherever you save it.

The exact wording we are held to is in the Privacy Policy and the Terms of Service. Both were re-checked against v1.0.14 source, not against marketing copy.

Connections

What each connection sends, and gets

Only connect a service if you want its features. Every request below also exposes your IP address and ordinary connection timing to that endpoint.

Spotify

OAuth with PKCE, no client secret. Search text and track lookups go to api.spotify.com; the preview that plays in-app is the track's own preview_url. Tokens live in Keystore-encrypted storage. Full playback stays in Spotify's app.

SoundCloud

OAuth 2.1 with PKCE against secure.soundcloud.com; search and stream resolution against api.soundcloud.com. Your Client ID and the client secret are stored encrypted on-device. SoundCloud requires every API client to hold a secret, so you must register an API app.

Jellyfin (your server)

Your address, username, and password go to that server; it decides what you may search, stream, and download. The password is never stored; the access token travels in a header, Keystore-encrypted on-device. Library records keep jellyfin:item:<id> references — not URLs or tokens.

YouTube / YouTube Music

Optional read-only youtube.readonly access to your playlists, and the one place Google services can matter. Connect Google account uses the sign-in API, so it needs Google Play services or a standard microG install — MicroG RE alone is not enough, because it only authenticates patched apps. Connect with Client ID signs in through your browser with a Desktop OAuth client and needs no Google services at all. Playlists import into one local liked list (bounded, matched on-device); Ask's catalogue search uses the same read-only connection. YouTube Music handoff, share-back, and audio extraction use no Google sign-in whatsoever.

TheAudioDB

Artist and title lookup goes to a free public API using its shared key; there is no account and the app stores nothing about you. If you switch on Look up genre & mood tags, the artist and title of the specific untagged songs a mix is unsure about are sent there, and the learned tags stay on the device. Off by default.

Mood model (optional)

Off by default; the on-device reading is the default. If you choose one, only the mood description (up to 400 characters), an energy hint, the local hour, whether you are moving, and the search-target sentence go to: an Ollama server you run, an OpenAI-compatible endpoint you type (a hosted API, a model on your own machine, or Arena's preset), or Google AI Studio. API keys and an Ollama proxy token are Keystore-encrypted, never synced, and excluded from exports. The model's song suggestions are only suggestions — each is searched for on the provider you chose before anything is played. Use of a model is subject to the provider's Terms of Service and policies, which you must follow; review its Privacy Policy before sending a mood request. Before enabling AI Mood—or after changing the provider or endpoint URL—verify which service will receive the request, review its current privacy, retention/training practices and any charges, and confirm you trust the destination. Soundtrail cannot verify who operates a custom endpoint or which policies apply. Avoid including sensitive or identifying details in mood descriptions. AI Mood is for music discovery only, not medical or mental-health advice, diagnosis, or crisis support.

Voice (microphone)

The Ask tab asks for the microphone only when you press it. The audio goes to the phone's chosen speech-recognition service — usually Google's, sometimes an offline engine — under that service's own policy; Soundtrail never records or keeps it and receives only the recognised words. Replies are spoken by the phone's text-to-speech engine, and the switch turns that off. Typing works without any microphone or speech service.

Android Auto

Android Auto browses pins, playlists and their songs through Android's media-browser API. The app exports that service because the car runs in another app, but it answers only the app itself, the car hosts (Android Auto, Android Automotive), the Google app, the system UI, and callers the platform already trusts with media control — identified by the uid the system reports. Provider tokens, the Jellyfin address and downloaded audio are never exposed, and the car path uploads nothing.

Maps and tiles

Raster tiles for the visible area come from basemaps.cartocdn.com. The tile coordinates reveal the area you are looking at; your pin records, notes, and exact coordinates are drawn locally and are not part of a tile request.

One connection is not the other. Signing in to the sync server, connecting a music provider, and switching on a mood model are independent choices. Turning on sync does not tell the server which artists you searched for, connecting Spotify does not upload your library, and a model reading never receives your songs — see the voice and AI section of the Privacy Policy for exactly what each endpoint receives.
Get it

Install Soundtrail

There is no Play Store listing: this is a self-distributed, debug-signed build, which is also why anyone can audit and rebuild it.

  1. Take the APK from a Release, or build it

    Every merged pull request bumps the patch version and publishes a GitHub Release with soundtrail-<version>-debug.apk attached; the newest is v1.0.14. Grab it from Releases — or build the same source yourself:

    git clone https://github.com/mob5824m-wq/SoundTrail.git
cd SoundTrail
./gradlew :app:assembleDebug

  2. Install it

    Sideload the APK directly, or with adb install soundtrail-1.0.14-debug.apk. Android 8.0 or newer is required; there is no Play Store listing and no Play services requirement for the app to run, so it installs on a de-Googled ROM. Grant location only if you want pins at your position — you can type coordinates instead. The single feature that wants Google services is the optional Google-account YouTube sign-in, which you can swap for the browser Client ID path. Voice input needs a speech recogniser on the phone (Google's engine or an offline one); typing always works.

  3. Expect one clean install if you came from an old build

    APKs built before the shared keystore/soundtrail-debug.p12 key were signed with a throwaway per-machine key, so they cannot be updated in place. Uninstall once, then install; the uninstall deletes local app data, so export a JSON backup and have your recovery key saved first. Every APK from this repository since v1.0.9 installs over the previous version.

  4. Use it in the car

    Android Auto lists pins and playlists from the phone. For a sideloaded build, open the Android Auto app → Settings → tap Version ten times to unlock Developer settings, then enable Unknown sources. Nothing is uploaded, and no Soundtrail account is involved.

  5. Optionally, point sync at your own server

    A fresh install connects to the public sync server (https://soundtrail.duckdns.org) for account creation and encrypted sync. The self-host guide covers a native Python setup behind HTTPS, its limits, and what to back up.

  6. Save the recovery key before you rely on sync

    The app generates a random 256-bit key when you create an account. Nothing else can decrypt your library afterwards: the server has no key-recovery service, and a password reset does not recover encrypted content.

Debug signing is deliberate. The published APKs are signed with the conventional public Android debug key committed in the repository, and no release signing is configured. That makes updates install in place, and it means these builds are meant for sideloading — they are not a hardened Play Store release and should not be treated as one.
FAQ

Questions people actually ask

Do I need Google Play services or a Google account?

Not to install the app, and not to use the local product. Pins, playlists, imported audio, offline files, automations, JSON export, in-app playback of your own files and Jellyfin streams, the on-device mood engine, Android Auto browsing of your library, and even the YouTube Music handoff all work with no Google sign-in. Three exceptions are worth naming. Connect Google account for YouTube playlist search and import goes through the Google sign-in client, so it needs Google Play services or a standard microG install under com.google.android.gms; MicroG RE and ReVanced GmsCore only authenticate patched apps, so use Connect with Client ID instead — it opens Google in your browser, stores its own tokens encrypted on-device, and needs nothing Google-shaped installed. Voice input uses whichever speech recogniser the phone has (usually Google's, sometimes an offline engine); typing is the no-service path. The optional mood models are plain HTTPS endpoints you choose, so they need no Play services. Android Auto itself is a Google app on the phone, but the library it reads stays local.

What does a mood model see?

Off by default. If you choose a model, only your mood description (up to 400 characters), an energy hint if you set one, the local hour, whether you are moving (only while the automation watch is on), and the sentence naming where to search are sent to the endpoint you picked: your own Ollama server, an OpenAI-compatible endpoint you type (which may be OpenAI, OpenRouter, a model on your machine, or Arena's preset), or Google AI Studio. Song titles, artists, playlists, pins, and places are never sent, and switching back to On-device stops all of it. API keys are Keystore-encrypted, never synced, and excluded from exports. The songs a model names are not trusted: each is searched for on the provider you chose, and a made-up song simply matches nothing.

What happens to what I say?

Soundtrail does not record or save audio, and it does not keep a transcript — apart from a mood description you use to build a mix, which stays with your local mood settings. Pressing the microphone asks Android for RECORD_AUDIO at that moment; the phone's chosen speech recogniser turns the phrase into words, and the app uses those words for the request in front of you. Depending on the recogniser, that audio may be processed on-device or by its provider (a standard Google recogniser can send it to Google) under that provider's own policy. For a song request, if a Google account is connected, the search phrase — the recognised words, not the audio — is sent to YouTube's Data API unless the request names another provider; matching against your own library stays on-device. Spoken replies use the phone's text-to-speech engine and can be switched off. You can always type instead.

What can Android Auto see?

The library on the phone: pins, playlists, the songs inside them, and what is currently playing. The car browses through Android's media-browser service, which the app must export for another app to reach; it answers only the app itself, the known car hosts, the Google app, the system UI, and callers the platform already trusts with media control, identified by uid. Tokens, the Jellyfin address, and downloaded audio are not exposed, and no account is needed. Voice search in the car matches only titles, artists, and places already saved on the phone.

Does the server ever see my pins, notes, or coordinates?

Not in plaintext. Each pin, playlist, playlist song, and automation is encrypted on your device before upload; the server stores opaque ciphertext envelopes plus sequence metadata, so it can see sizes, counts, and timing — not contents or your recovery key. Your account email, IP address, and traffic metadata are not hidden by end-to-end encryption, and the host or reverse proxy may log them. Locally stored audio, provider tokens, mood settings, and model keys are excluded from sync entirely.

Can I use Soundtrail with no account at all?

Yes. Dropping pins, building playlists, attaching audio, exporting JSON, running automations, mood mixes on the device, Ask against your own library, and Android Auto all work locally. An account is only needed for sync and for the optional YouTube audio-extraction endpoint, and both have a local alternative (JSON export, and attaching files you already own).

What happens if I lose the recovery key?

Encrypted server-side data becomes unreadable, on this and any future device. The key can be revealed again on a device that is already signed in, but a new device needs the key itself, and uninstalling removes that device's stored copy. There is no reset path, by design: a server that could hand back your key could also read your library.

Why is my automation late, or why did it not fire?

Motion is inferred from GPS speed bands (stationary under 0.5 m/s, walking to about 2.6 m/s, running to 7.5 m/s, driving above that), so tunnels, indoor use, and poor fixes delay or miss triggers. Rules re-arm on a 15-minute cooldown rather than edge triggering, so a rule can fire again while conditions still hold. Continuous GPS also uses noticeably more battery, which is why the watch only runs while its toggle is on and its notification is visible.

Is my JSON export encrypted?

No, and that is the point of it being portable. An export is readable JSON containing pins, coordinates, notes, playlists, song links, and automation rules — no login credentials, no AI keys, no audio bytes. Protect the file and delete copies you do not need. Encrypted sync and JSON export are separate mechanisms with different guarantees.

Does Soundtrail show ads, sell data, or track me?

There is no advertising ID, no ad SDK, no general analytics SDK, and no crash reporter in the app; the only third-party service SDK in the dependency list is Google's sign-in library, used for the optional YouTube connection. Mood readings are not an SDK: they are plain HTTPS requests to a provider you choose and switch on. Your library is not sold or used for advertising. Google processes sign-in activity under its own policy, and Soundtrail's use of Google API data follows the Google API Services User Data Policy, including Limited Use.

Can it download any song I want?

No. Jellyfin downloads go through your own server's authenticated endpoint and obey that account's permissions. The optional YouTube extractor is unofficial, uses no cookies or login session, and only attempts a publicly accessible audio stream — private, age-restricted, gated, or DRM-protected items fail. Both paths are capped at 250 MiB per track, and you must confirm you own the content or may download it before the request is sent. The same rules apply to a YouTube result found by Ask or a mood mix.

Why is it not on the Play Store, and is it safe to sideload?

A Play release needs a private signing key and a review process the project has deliberately not set up. Sideload only from the repository's Releases, check that the version matches the tag you read, and remember that a debug-signed build is still a debug build: useful, auditable, and open to modification by anyone who has the source.

Recent changes

What moved most recently

Re-checked against main at 37b4413 on October 18, 2026, at v1.0.14. Every merged pull request bumps the patch version and publishes a Release, so check the commit list for anything newer.

Ask

Talk to it, hear the answer

The Ask tab takes a request by voice or keyboard, strips command words without mangling a title, puts its on-device library matches ahead of a YouTube catalogue search when a Google account is connected. Results are queued in the one player, and replies can be spoken or switched to text-only.

Mood

From a feeling to a mix

The Mood tab builds a mix from your own pins and playlists with an explainable on-device engine, then optionally asks a model you choose — Ollama, any OpenAI-compatible endpoint, Arena's preset, or Google AI Studio — and verifies every song it names against a real provider search.

Car

Android Auto

Pins and playlists are browsable in the car, with next/previous and a scrubber, through the same player and queue as the phone and the notification. The exported browse service refuses callers that are not the car host, the system's media surfaces, or trusted media-control apps.

Library

One likes list, and a map you can aim

YouTube imports now consolidate into the liked list you already have, with duplicate links and offline copies handled. The map gained search, a Me button and a Pin action, and a Spotify result can be opened in one tap from Ask.

Releases

APKs from the new repository

Releases v1.0.9 through v1.0.14 each attach soundtrail-<version>-debug.apk. They share the committed debug key, so each installs over the last; an APK from before that key still needs one clean install.

Honesty

What the app does not claim

YouTube Music's account library is not open to other apps, so songs are looked up by name and a YT Music button opens the real one. A model's suggestion is never played just because a model named it, and a YouTube download still needs your rights confirmation.
