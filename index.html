Your music, pinned to
the places you love.

Soundtrail saves Spotify, SoundCloud, YouTube Music, and Jellyfin songs as private pins on your own map — with local playlists, offline audio, opt-in place/motion/time automations, and end-to-end encrypted sync you can self-host. Everything stays on your device unless you turn a sync or provider connection on.

Features

What the app does

Each feature below is opt-in except the local library. Nothing described here requires an account or a server.

Map

Pins on your own map

An in-app OpenStreetMap-based map draws every pin in its provider colour. Tap for the song card, long-press anywhere to drop a new pin, and switch List ↔ Map at any time.

Providers

Your existing music, not a new store

Search Spotify, SoundCloud, your Jellyfin server, or your own YouTube playlists, or paste a supported link. The free TheAudioDB lookup fills in artist, title, album, and a link with no account at all.

Playback

In-app player with device controls

Jellyfin streams, your imported files, Spotify previews, SoundCloud streams, and downloaded YouTube audio play in-app, with a notification and lockscreen/Bluetooth controls. Full-length Spotify tracks still open in Spotify's own app.

Offline

Audio you can keep

Attach an audio file you already own, or download an eligible Jellyfin track through its authenticated endpoint (250 MiB per track). Offline storage is yours to audit: per-file sizes, largest first, remove one or clear all.

Automations

Playlists that notice where you are

Point a playlist at conditions: near a saved spot (20–2000 m), while walking, running, or driving, and/or between two times of day. An opt-in foreground watch posts a notification when a rule matches — it never plays audio without a tap.

Sync

Encrypted, and yours to host

Optional two-way sync of pins, playlists, links, and automations, encrypted on-device with AES-256-GCM under a recovery key only you hold. The public server works out of the box; a self-hosted server takes one URL change.

Portability

Plain-JSON backup

Export pins, coordinates, notes, playlists, song links, and automation rules to a JSON file you choose; import merges them back additively. No proprietary cloud format, no lock-in.

Sharing

Share-back from your music app

Share a song from Spotify, YouTube Music (including Morphe), or SoundCloud into Soundtrail and the pin editor opens prefilled. Soundtrail never reads another app's storage or session.

Under the hood

Works on a de-Googled phone

Motion is estimated from GPS speed rather than an activity-recognition API, the whole local library works with the network switched off, and installing needs no Play Store account. One optional feature does want Google services: the Google-account sign-in for YouTube playlist search, which needs Play services or standard microG. The Client ID option skips them entirely.

Your data

Two directions, and you control each one

The local library is the default and the whole product. The two optional paths that move information — provider connections and cloud sync — are separate switches with different data flows.

Stays on the device

  • Pins, titles, artists, notes, coordinates, place labels, and creation times.
  • Playlists, imported audio, and downloaded offline files, in private app storage.
  • Automation rules, the live watch state, and provider tokens (Android Keystore-encrypted).
  • Search matching against your YouTube playlists, which happens on-device.

Leaves the device only when you enable it

  • Provider search/playback: your query, track IDs, and ordinary request metadata reach Spotify, SoundCloud, TheAudioDB, Google's YouTube Data API, or your Jellyfin server.
  • Cloud sync: encrypted library records reach the server you selected; your recovery key and audio files never do.
  • Map tiles: the visible area reaches the tile server, because tiles are fetched for the area you look at.
  • Exports: a readable JSON file goes wherever you save it.

The exact wording we are held to is in the Privacy Policy and the Terms of Service. Both were re-checked against v1.0.8 source, not against marketing copy.

Connections

What each connection sends, and gets

Only connect a service if you want its features. Every request below also exposes your IP address and ordinary connection timing to that endpoint.

Spotify

OAuth with PKCE, no client secret. Search text and track lookups go to api.spotify.com; the preview that plays in-app is the track's own preview_url. Tokens live in Keystore-encrypted storage. Full playback stays in Spotify's app.

SoundCloud

OAuth 2.1 with PKCE against secure.soundcloud.com; search and stream resolution against api.soundcloud.com. Your Client ID and the client secret are stored encrypted on-device. SoundCloud requires every API client to hold a secret, so you must register an API app.

Jellyfin (your server)

Your address, username, and password go to that server; it decides what you may search, stream, and download. The password is never stored; the access token travels in a header, Keystore-encrypted on-device. Library records keep jellyfin:item:<id> references — not URLs or tokens.

YouTube / YouTube Music

Optional read-only youtube.readonly access to your playlists, and the one place Google services can matter. Connect Google account uses the sign-in API, so it needs Google Play services or a standard microG install — MicroG RE alone is not enough, because it only authenticates patched apps. Connect with Client ID signs in through your browser with a Desktop OAuth client and needs no Google services at all. Playlists import into your local library (bounded, matched on-device); YouTube Music handoff, share-back, and audio extraction use no Google sign-in whatsoever.

TheAudioDB

The artist and title you type are sent to a free public API using its shared key; there is no account and nothing is stored about you. Results can fill a pin, and any link it offers passes the same allowlist as a pasted one.

Maps and tiles

Raster tiles for the visible area come from basemaps.cartocdn.com. The tile coordinates reveal the area you are looking at; your pin records, notes, and exact coordinates are drawn locally and are not part of a tile request.

One connection is not the other. Signing in to the sync server and connecting a music provider are independent choices. Turning on sync does not tell the server which artists you searched for, and connecting Spotify does not upload your library — see the sync section of the Privacy Policy for exactly what the server can and cannot see.
Get it

Install Soundtrail

There is no Play Store listing: this is a self-distributed, debug-signed build, which is also why anyone can audit and rebuild it.

  1. Take the APK from a Release, or build it

    Every pull request merged into main bumps the patch version and publishes a GitHub Release with soundtrail-<version>-debug.apk attached. Grab it from Releases — or build the same source yourself:

    git clone https://github.com/mob5824m-wq/SoundTrail.git
cd SoundTrail
./gradlew :app:assembleDebug

  2. Install it

    Sideload the APK directly, or with adb install soundtrail-1.0.8-debug.apk. Android 8.0 or newer is required; there is no Play Store listing and no Play services requirement for the app to run, so it installs on a de-Googled ROM. Grant location only if you want pins at your position — you can type coordinates instead. The single feature that wants Google services is the optional Google-account YouTube sign-in, which you can swap for the browser Client ID path.

  3. Expect one clean install if you came from an old build

    APKs built before the shared keystore/soundtrail-debug.p12 key were signed with a throwaway per-machine key, so they cannot be updated in place. Uninstall once, then install; the uninstall deletes local app data, so export a JSON backup and have your recovery key saved first. Everything built from this repository since then installs over the previous version.

  4. Optionally, point sync at your own server

    A fresh install connects to the public sync server (https://soundtrail.duckdns.org) for account creation and encrypted sync. The self-host guide covers a native Python setup behind HTTPS, its limits, and what to back up.

  5. Save the recovery key before you rely on sync

    The app generates a random 256-bit key when you create an account. Nothing else can decrypt your library afterwards: the server has no key-recovery service, and a password reset does not recover encrypted content.

Debug signing is deliberate. The published APKs are signed with the conventional public Android debug key committed in the repository, and no release signing is configured. That makes updates install in place, and it means these builds are meant for sideloading — they are not a hardened Play Store release and should not be treated as one.
FAQ

Questions people actually ask

Do I need Google Play services or a Google account?

Not to install the app, and not to use it. Pins, playlists, imported audio, offline files, automations, JSON export, in-app playback of your own files and Jellyfin streams, and even the YouTube Music handoff (open a search, open your library, share a song back) all work with no Google sign-in and no Play services. One optional feature has a dependency: Connect Google account for YouTube playlist search and import goes through the Google sign-in client, so it needs Google Play services or a standard microG install under com.google.android.gms. MicroG RE and ReVanced GmsCore only authenticate patched apps, so they do not help here — use Connect with Client ID instead. It opens Google in your browser with a Desktop OAuth client you create, stores its own tokens encrypted on-device, and needs nothing Google-shaped installed. Signing in inside YouTube Music or Morphe is that app's business; Soundtrail never reads its session, and the audio extractor on your sync server uses no Google token or cookie at all.

Does the server ever see my pins, notes, or coordinates?

Not in plaintext. Each pin, playlist, playlist song, and automation is encrypted on your device before upload; the server stores opaque ciphertext envelopes plus sequence metadata, so it can see sizes, counts, and timing — not contents or your recovery key. Your account email, IP address, and traffic metadata are not hidden by end-to-end encryption, and the host or reverse proxy may log them. Locally stored audio and provider tokens are excluded from sync entirely.

Can I use Soundtrail with no account at all?

Yes. Dropping pins, building playlists, attaching audio, exporting JSON, and running automations are all local. An account is only needed for sync and for the optional YouTube audio-extraction endpoint, and both have a local alternative (JSON export, and attaching files you already own).

What happens if I lose the recovery key?

Encrypted server-side data becomes unreadable, on this and any future device. The key can be revealed again on a device that is already signed in, but a new device needs the key itself, and uninstalling removes that device's stored copy. There is no reset path, by design: a server that could hand back your key could also read your library.

Why is my automation late, or why did it not fire?

Motion is inferred from GPS speed bands (stationary under 0.5 m/s, walking to about 2.6 m/s, running to 7.5 m/s, driving above that), so tunnels, indoor use, and poor fixes delay or miss triggers. Rules re-arm on a 15-minute cooldown rather than edge triggering, so a rule can fire again while conditions still hold. Continuous GPS also uses noticeably more battery, which is why the watch only runs while its toggle is on and its notification is visible.

Is my JSON export encrypted?

No, and that is the point of it being portable. An export is readable JSON containing pins, coordinates, notes, playlists, song links, and automation rules — no login credentials, no audio bytes. Protect the file and delete copies you do not need. Encrypted sync and JSON export are separate mechanisms with different guarantees.

Does Soundtrail show ads, sell data, or track me?

There is no advertising ID, no ad SDK, no general analytics SDK, and no crash reporter in the app; the only third-party SDK in the dependency list is Google's sign-in library, used for the optional YouTube connection. Your library is not sold or used for advertising. Google processes sign-in activity under its own policy, and Soundtrail's use of Google API data follows the Google API Services User Data Policy, including Limited Use.

Can it download any song I want?

No. Jellyfin downloads go through your own server's authenticated endpoint and obey that account's permissions. The optional YouTube extractor is unofficial, uses no cookies or login session, and only attempts a publicly accessible audio stream — private, age-restricted, gated, or DRM-protected items fail. Both paths are capped at 250 MiB per track, and you must confirm you own the content or may download it before the request is sent.

Why is it not on the Play Store, and is it safe to sideload?

A Play release needs a private signing key and a review process the project has deliberately not set up. Sideload only from the repository's Releases, check that the version matches the tag you read, and remember that a debug-signed build is still a debug build: useful, auditable, and open to modification by anyone who has the source.

Self-hosting

No Docker needed

The sync server is plain Python started with python3 run.py, which builds its own virtualenv; it runs on Linux, macOS, Windows, and BSD on any CPU, defaults to loopback behind your TLS proxy, and ships a native test command. Guide →

Sign-in

Google without Google services

A Desktop OAuth Client ID path signs in through the browser with PKCE and no secret, so the optional YouTube connection works on MicroG RE and on devices with no Play services at all. A shared committed debug key also means each release APK installs over the last one.
